The Dajjal, also known as Al-Masih ad-Dajjal (the False Messiah), is a central antagonistic figure in Islamic eschatology, prophesied to emerge before the Day of Judgment. He is not mentioned in the Quran but is extensively described in numerous Hadith (sayings and traditions of the Prophet Muhammad). The term "Dajjal" derives from Arabic roots meaning "lie," "deception," "to cover," or "to gild," reflecting his nature as a great deceiver who conceals truth with falsehood.

**Background:**
The concept of the Dajjal draws parallels with the Antichrist in Christian eschatology. His appearance is considered one of the ten major signs signaling the impending end of the world. Prophecies indicate that he will appear during a period of great tribulation and will lead many astray through his extraordinary, albeit false, displays of power.

**Key Facts:**
The Dajjal is typically described as a young man with a ruddy complexion, short stature, thick curly hair, a wide forehead, and a broad upper chest. A distinguishing physical characteristic is his blindness or defectiveness in one eye, often likened to a "floating grape." Crucially, the Arabic letters "K-F-R" (Kafir, meaning 'disbeliever') will be inscribed on his forehead, readable by all true believers, whether literate or illiterate. He is also said to be sterile, having no children.

The Dajjal will claim to be the promised Messiah and eventually god. He will perform astonishing "miracles" that deceive many, such as healing the sick, raising the dead (with the aid of demons), commanding the sky to rain, causing the earth to grow vegetation, making livestock prosper, and even appearing to stop the sun's movement. He will bring with him what he calls "paradise" and "hell," though his paradise will be hell and his hell, paradise. He will possess abundant food and water during a time of famine. His reign on Earth will last for 40 days, with the first day being as long as a year, the second as a month, the third as a week, and the remaining days like normal ones. He is expected to emerge from the East, though specific locations vary, sometimes mentioned as between the Levant and Iraq, or Khorasan. His followers will include a significant number of Jews, who will see him as their awaited savior, along with Bedouins, weavers, magicians, and those of illegitimate birth. Ultimately, the Dajjal will be defeated and killed by Isa (Jesus), who will descend near a white minaret east of Damascus, and/or the Mahdi (the "rightly guided one").

**Primary Controversies:**
A major point of contention revolves around the interpretation of the Dajjal. While many Muslims adhere to a literal understanding of him as a single, physical individual, others interpret the Dajjal metaphorically. This metaphorical view suggests he represents evil forces, materialism, spiritual blindness, or specific socio-political movements, such as Zionism or Western cultural dominance. The Ahmadiyya Muslim Community, for instance, understands Dajjal as an intangible concept embodying "the great deceiver" and satanic forces, often linked to the historical rise of Christian clergy and Western materialistic culture. Another controversy stems from the absence of the Dajjal in the Quran, leading some scholars to consider his figure less significant, despite his pervasive presence in Hadith literature. There are also scholarly differences regarding the exact chronology of end-time events, particularly the precise sequence of the Mahdi's emergence relative to the Dajjal.

**Why Trending and Future Outlook:**
The Dajjal remains a potent symbol and a source of both fear and fascination within Islamic eschatology. Discussions about the Dajjal often trend during times of global unrest, as believers seek to connect contemporary geopolitical chaos, economic systems (like "bogus monetary systems"), and the rise of various global powers (sometimes referred to as "Pax Judaica") to the prophecies surrounding him. This ongoing interpretation of current events through the lens of Dajjal prophecies underscores its enduring relevance and the belief that his arrival is intrinsically linked to the broader End Times narrative, including the advent of the Mahdi and the return of Jesus Christ. The Dajjal is seen as the "biggest fitnah" (trial) mankind will ever face, ensuring continued vigilance and discussion among Muslims regarding the signs of his coming and how to seek refuge from his deception.
